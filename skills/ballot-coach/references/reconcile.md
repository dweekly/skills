# Reconciling research with beliefs

Inputs: the research reports and the beliefs file, including guide-trust ratings and votes from
past elections. If they exist, past votes are the strongest evidence of how this person actually
weighs things, so use them.

## Per item

1. **Applicable beliefs.** Quote them, with where each came from: this interview, the beliefs
   file section, or a past vote. Several may apply.
2. **Direction.** Say which way each belief points, and why.
3. **Alignment label:**
   - **Strong:** the user's beliefs agree and point clearly one way.
   - **Lean:** they point one way, with a real counterweight.
   - **Your call:** the user's beliefs say nothing that applies. Give the deciding question
     instead.
   - **Conflicted:** two of the user's beliefs point opposite ways. Name both.
4. **Tipping point.** The single fact or value judgment that would flip the result, phrased as a
   question the user can answer. Examples: "Do you think the earmarked half will be wasted or
   just less efficient?" or "Is a recall for firing a person or for changing direction?" If a
   checkable fact would settle it, say what it is and check it.
5. **Guides.** List what each guide recommends, tagged with the user's trust rating. Call out
   when a guide the user trusts disagrees with where the user's beliefs point; that's worth a
   second look in one direction or the other. Never let guide weight override the user's
   beliefs; it only flags items for a second look.
6. **Inference labels.** If the mapping depends on your inference rather than the user's words
   (for example, "a demand subsidy raises prices" is economics, not their statement), label it.

## Headline section (first thing the user reads)

- items whose ballot label misleads;
- measures that interact with each other;
- corrections to anything the user assumed in the interview;
- the close calls, ranked by how close they are.

## Tone

Plain language and no party framing. Don't recommend a vote on an item marked Your call. Keep
the full analysis per item to about 150 words; put detail behind an expandable section in the
HTML.
