# Beliefs interview

Goal: learn the handful of values that will actually decide this ballot, in the user's words, in
the time they chose. Ask tradeoffs ("given X costs Y, would you…"), not slogans ("do you support
education?"). Use AskUserQuestion for quick calibration, but let them answer most things in free
text.

## Time budgets

- **10 minutes:** sections A, B and E, plus the two or three topics from C that matter most to
  this ballot. Skip D unless there's time left.
- **20 minutes:** all of A–E, with one follow-up per answer.
- **30 minutes:** all of A–E, and probe every tension you notice.

Watch the ballot list (you have it already) and spend time on topics that are actually on this
ballot. Skip topics the ballot doesn't touch, unless the user wants a complete beliefs file for
future use.

## A. Foundations (always)

1. What is government for, in a sentence or two? Where should it stay out?
2. When you think a policy has failed, what should happen? Fix it, cut it, or give it time?
3. How much do you trust each of these to spend money well: your city, your county, the state,
   the federal government? Ask for a rough 1–5 rating each.
4. How do you usually decide, and does party matter to you? Ask honestly: "Is there a party whose
   slate you'd mostly follow, and how do you feel about that?"

## B. Money (always: most ballots are mostly money)

5. Taxes: who should pay more and who less? Rank income, sales, property, wealth and business
   taxes from most to least fair, and say why.
6. Borrowing: when is a bond worth it? What would make you vote no on one even for a good cause?
7. Locking spending in place: how do you feel about measures that earmark money or require a
   two-thirds vote, versus leaving it to legislators?

## C. Policy areas (pick by ballot)

Ask one or two tradeoff questions per area that the ballot touches.

- **Housing:** build more versus protect current residents; rent control; local control versus
  state mandates; environmental review.
- **Education:** what schools should prioritize first; testing; parents' role; school choice;
  funding.
- **Public safety:** prevention versus enforcement; sentencing; police oversight.
- **Health care:** public coverage; cost control; who should pay.
- **Climate and energy:** speed versus cost; permitting; nuclear and gas.
- **Transit and roads:** subsidize transit or let ridership decide; who pays.
- **Elections and democracy:** voter ID; mail voting; redistricting; campaign money; recalls;
  term limits.
- **Labor and business:** minimum wage; unions; regulation versus growth.
- **Immigration:** if any state or local measure touches it.
- **Social issues:** abortion, guns, LGBTQ policy, and others the ballot touches. Handle these
  respectfully and briefly; the user's view stands as stated.
- **Judges:** retain by default, or judge each one individually? What would make you vote no?

## D. Candidates (if there are contested races)

8. What matters more in a candidate: positions, competence, character, or party control of the
   body? Ask them to rank these.
9. Is there anyone you would never vote for, or a kind of past conduct you treat as disqualifying?
10. If you dislike both candidates, would you vote for the lesser evil, write someone in, or
    leave it blank?

## E. Voter guides and sources (always)

Show the list from voter-guides.md that fits this jurisdiction, plus any the user names. For each,
ask how persuasive it is (1–5) and why: "agree with their values", "trust their research",
"they're usually against what I want", and so on. Note that a low score can still be useful as a
contrary signal.

## Challenge pass (always, at the end)

Pick two or three of these and reflect them back, quoting the user:

- contradictions between answers (for example "tax the rich" alongside support for a sales tax);
- strong claims stated as fact without evidence;
- values that will collide on a specific ballot item;
- positions where the strongest opposing argument hasn't been considered. Give that argument
  fairly.

Ask whether they want to revise. Record the original, the challenge and the revision.
